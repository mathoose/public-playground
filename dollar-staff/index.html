Preview only. The socket is the broom head. The snap case is the top of the vertical stroke, the same thickness and the same depth, so the card does not stick out past the sign.

Dollar sign

One solid dollar sign: a single S with the vertical stroke through it. The free end of the S prints on the case back, so it is not a loose piece. The S is four times as thick along the card, and the stroke is deepened out to the case so the box stays flush. The bottom corners of the case are sharp squares. Width grows the S.

Card slot

ISO ID-1 card, 85.60 × 53.98 × 0.76 mm, rotated around the broom so the card is perpendicular to the S. The long side stays vertical, the stroke crosses the middle, and the top of the card lines up with the top of the stroke. Clearance is extra gap on each side and on the thickness. Drop the card in the back, snap the front on the lip, then slide the case down the bar until the fin clicks.

Broom socket

Right-hand 3/4″-5 ACME female thread at the bottom, where a screw-off broom head would go. The dollar sign and the card sit up on the neck, not down beside the handle. Turn the head clockwise onto the stick. Engagement is how far that thread runs up the socket.

Case front Case back Dollar sign Socket test 3MF

Print the dollar sign with the socket mouth on the bed so the thread is vertical. Print the case back with it — the free end of the S is part of that piece. Print the case front flat. Drop the card in the back, snap the front on the lip, then slide the case down the bar until the fin clicks. The head sits up on the neck; the handle ends in the socket. A plain wooden dowel with no thread will not hold — this socket is threaded, not a slip sleeve. Cut a 3/4″-5 ACME on the stick, or use a broom or mop handle that already has that thread. The socket test is only that female thread, with no neck and no dollar sign, so you can try it on the broom before printing the sign.
